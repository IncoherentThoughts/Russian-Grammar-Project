If the past tense of the verb does not end **-л**, add **-ший** (without the **в**) to the masculine singular of the past tense:

**принести́**<sup>p</sup> 'to bring' (past **принёс**) – **принёсший**<sup>p</sup> 'who brought'

**Exceptions**

The main one is **идти́** (and all its prefixed forms) which has the past participle form **ше́дший**:

**войти́**<sup>p</sup> — **воше́дший**<sup>p</sup> 'who went in'
**пройти́**<sup>p</sup> — **проше́дший**<sup>p</sup> 'who went through'

Note also:

**вести́**<sup>i</sup> (to lead) — **ве́дший**<sup>i</sup> 'who/which led'
**приобрести́**<sup>p</sup> (to acquire) — **приобре́тший**<sup>p</sup> 'who acquired'

These participles always correspond in meaning to **кото́рый** + the past tense:

**Бизнесме́нов, изучи́<u>вших</u> ру́сский язы́к, о́чень ма́ло = Бизнесме́нов, кото́рые изучи́<u>ли</u> ру́сский язы́к, о́чень ма́ло.**

### 29.5 Participles as Adjectives: 'Has been Wife'

A number of active participles, mainly present ones, are commonly used as adjectives (i.e. they are used in front of a noun and have little or no verbal meaning of action). They are listed in dictionaries.
