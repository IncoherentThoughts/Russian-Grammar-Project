(c) Verbs denoting states rather than actions are normally imperfective. О́н был<sup>i</sup> до́ма 'He was at home', Они́ жи́ли<sup>i</sup> в Москве́ 'They lived in Moscow'.

(d) Expressions of duration ('all day', 'for three hours') require the imperfective.

Я писа́л<sup>i</sup> весь день 'I wrote/was writing all day'. This sentence does not indicate how much I wrote or whether I finished what I was writing, so it has to be imperfective. You will find that duration phrases such 'for a long time' (до́лго), 'for three hours' (три часа́), 'all night' (всю но́чь) always go with imperfective verbs.[^1]

(e) If there was no action, use the imperfective.

Она́ не звони́ла<sup>i</sup>. — She didn't phone.

(f) Actions attempted but without result are imperfective.

Я вам звони́ла<sup>i</sup>. — I telephoned you (but you weren't in).

**Perfective<sup>(p)</sup>**

(i) Use the perfective for completed single events.

Мы пошли́<sup>p</sup> домо́й. — We went home.

(ii) Sequences of actions, except when they are repeated actions (see (b) above) are perfective, since each action must be finished before the next one can take place.

Я позвони́ла<sup>p</sup> и сказа́ла<sup>p</sup>, что́ приду́. — I phoned and said I'd come.

(iii) With negation (не) the perfective indicates failure to do something.

Она́ не позвони́ла<sup>p</sup>. — She failed to phone (though she was expected to).

[^1]: Except for perfective verbs with the prefix про- meaning 'to spend a specified amount of time doing something' e.g. Мы про́жили<sup>p</sup> там три го́да 'We lived there for three years.'
