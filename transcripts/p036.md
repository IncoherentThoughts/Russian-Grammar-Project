(c) Neuter nouns end -о or -е:

окно́ 'window'
вино́ 'wine'
упражне́ние 'exercise' [oo-prazh-nyé-nee-ye]

So you can normally tell the gender of a noun just by looking at it.
However, there are awkward cases:

(d) Most nouns ending -ь (soft sign) are feminine, but there are many masculine ones too, so you have to learn the gender of soft-sign nouns.

Feminine examples:

Сиби́рь 'Siberia' [see-be'erʸ]
мать 'mother' [matʸ]
дверь 'door' [dvyerʸ]

Masculine examples:

рубль 'rouble' [rooblʸ]
И́горь 'Igor' [e'e-garʸ]
день 'day' [dyenʸ]

(e) Nouns ending -а or -я which denote males are masculine:
па́па 'father', 'dad'
Воло́дя 'Volodia' (a familiar form of Vladimir)
дя́дя 'uncle'

(f) If a noun ends -и or -у or -ю, it is likely to be a foreign borrowing and to be _neuter_:

такси́ 'taxi'
меню́ 'menu'

(g) Foreign words denoting females are feminine, whatever their endings:

ле́ди (f) 'lady'
Мэ́ри (f) 'Mary'
Ма́ргарет (f) 'Margaret'
