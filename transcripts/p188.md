Кто́ стро́ил<sup>i</sup> Большо́й теа́тр? — Who built the Bolshoi Theatre? (I want to know the name of the architect, not to stress the fact that the theatre was finished)

The perfective is used:

(9) to describe an action as a completed whole, with a beginning and an end:

В суббо́ту я купи́л<sup>p</sup> «Пра́вду». — I bought _Pravda_ on Saturday.
Я э́то сде́лаю<sup>p</sup> за́втра. — I'll do it tomorrow.
Да́йте<sup>p</sup> два биле́та, пожа́луйста. — Would you give me two tickets?
Я хочу́ купи́ть<sup>p</sup> слова́рь. — I want to buy a dictionary.

(10) to indicate the relevance of the result:

Вы купи́ли<sup>p</sup> «Пра́вду» сего́дня? — Have you bought _Pravda_ today? (I.e. perhaps I want to borrow it)

(11) to indicate completion, particularly in sequences of actions, where each action must be finished before the next one can begin:

Вади́м пришёл<sup>p</sup> домо́й, вы́пил<sup>p</sup> воды́ и сел<sup>p</sup> у окна́. — Vadim came home, drank some water and sat down by the window.

(12) with infinitives, to show that something is impossible or can't be done:

Откры́ть<sup>p</sup> окно́ невозмо́жно. — It's impossible to open the window.
Она́ не могла́ найти́<sup>p</sup> такси́. — She couldn't find a taxi.

(13) with negation (не) in the past tense, to indicate failure to do something:

Ива́н хоте́л откры́ть окно́, но́ не смо́г<sup>p</sup>. — Ivan wanted to open the window but failed.
Она́ сказа́ла, что́ придёт, но́ (так и) не пришла́<sup>p</sup>. — She said she'd come but (in fact) she didn't.
