_Note:_ Peter's city on the Neva, founded 1703, was officially Санкт-Петербу́рг until the First World War, though the name was often shortened to Петербу́рг. Санкт 'saint', Петер 'Peter' and бург 'town' are all German borrowings; the Russian equivalents would be свято́й, Пётр and го́род or град. During the War, in 1914, the German-sounding name was Russified into Петрогра́д. In 1924, after Lenin's death, the city acquired its Communist-era name Ленингра́д.
