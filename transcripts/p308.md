Remember that a Russian negative sentence contains _one_ **не**, so do not try to put another **не** before the verb.

As with **никто́** etc., the other case forms are based on the forms of **кто́** and **что́** (see 24.6) and any preposition comes between the **не́** and the form of **кто́** or **что́**:

**<u>Не́</u> с <u>ке́м</u> поговори́ть.** — There is no one to talk to (with).
**<u>Не́</u> от <u>кого́</u> получа́ть пи́сем.** — There is no one from whom (I) could
  — receive letters.

The future is formed with **бу́дет** and the past with **бы́ло**:

**Нам <u>не́</u> о <u>че́м</u> бы́ло говори́ть.** — We had nothing to talk about.
  — (No dots on **чем** because stress on **не́**)

The other words in the set are **не́где** 'there is nowhere' (place), **не́куда** 'there is nowhere' (motion) and **не́когда** 'there is no time', based on **где́** 'where', **куда́** 'where'/'whither', and **когда́** 'when'.

**Не́где се́сть.** — There is nowhere to sit down.
**Нам не́куда идти́.** — There is nowhere for us to go.
  — (We have nowhere to go.)
**Идти́ бы́ло не́куда.** — There was nowhere to go.
**Мне́ не́когда.** — I haven't got time.
