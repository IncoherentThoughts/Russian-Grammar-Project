(2) for uncompleted actions, so that English continuous forms ('I was doing', 'She will be working') always correspond to the imperfective:

Мы шли<sup>i</sup> домо́й. — We were walking home.
Я бу́ду чита́ть<sup>i</sup> весь ве́чер. — I shall be reading all evening.

(3) for states and processes:

О́н жил<sup>i</sup> в Москве́. — He lived in Moscow.
Мэ́ри изуча́ла<sup>i</sup> ру́сский язы́к. — Mary studied Russian.
Мы бу́дем жить<sup>i</sup> у друзе́й. — We'll stay with friends.

(4) for negated actions (particularly in the past), to indicate absence of action:

Она́ не приходи́ла<sup>i</sup>. — She didn't come.

(5) with infinitives and the negated imperative (15.3, rule 3) to indicate undesirability of an action:

Не на́до покупа́ть<sup>i</sup> э́ту кни́гу. — You shouldn't buy that book.
Не покупа́йте<sup>i</sup> э́ту кни́гу. — Don't buy that book.
Я сове́тую вам не покупа́ть<sup>i</sup> её. — I advise you not to buy it.

(6) for actions repeated an unspecified number of times:

Я всегда́ звони́ла<sup>i</sup> в семь часо́в. — I always rang at seven.
Я бу́ду писа́ть<sup>i</sup> ча́сто. — I shall write often.
Пиши́те<sup>i</sup> ча́сто. — Write often.

(7) in the past tense, to indicate that the result of an action was cancelled or annulled – particularly with verbs of motion (13.3):

Вчера́ приходи́л<sup>i</sup> мо́й ру́сский друг. — My Russian friend came yesterday (and left again).
Я открыва́л<sup>i</sup> окно́. — I had the window open (it's closed now).

(8) when the fact of completion is irrelevant:

Вы чита́ли<sup>i</sup> «Войну́ и мир»? — Have you read _War and Peace_? (I'm not concerned with whether you finished it)
