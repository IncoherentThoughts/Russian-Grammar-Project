In Russian, when someone's words are reported, the tense (past, present or future) of the original words stays unchanged. So if Mary says Я говорю́ (pres.) по-ру́сски, then any report of her words ('Mary said that she spoke Russian') will keep the present tense (Мэ́ри сказа́ла, что́ она́ говори́т по-ру́сски). English grammar is more complicated here. Notice how in English a future tense statement (e.g. Vadim: I shall phone Vera) acquires a 'would' form if it is reported: ('Vadim said that he _would_ phone Vera'). In Russian the future tense remains.

Вади́м: Я позвоню́ Ве́ре.

Reported speech: Вади́м сказа́л, что́ о́н позвони́т (fut.) Ве́ре.

Other examples:

Vera: I was (past) in London.
Report: Vera said that she had been (pluperfect) in London.
Ве́ра: Я была́ (past) в Ло́ндоне.
Report: Ве́ра сказа́ла, что́ она́ была́ (past) в Ло́ндоне.

This preference for keeping the tense of the original words is extended to cover seeing, hearing and other forms of perception in addition to verbs of speaking:

I heard you were now living (i.e. are living) in Siberia.
Я слы́шал, что́ вы сейча́с живёте в Сиби́ри.

I knew he was Russian.
Я знал, что́ о́н ру́сский ('I knew that he _is_ Russian').

We thought he was in Berlin.
Мы ду́мали, что́ о́н в Берли́не (pres.).

Vera realized that he was annoyed (i.e. that he is annoyed).
Ве́ра поняла́, что́ о́н се́рдится (pres.).
