**Rule 4**: Use the _imperfective_ for invitations.

Приходи́те<sup>i</sup> к нам. — Do come and see us.
Бери́те<sup>i</sup> ещё — Take some more (food).
Сади́тесь<sup>i</sup>. — Take a seat.

### EXERCISE 15/4

Translate and account for the choice of aspect:

1 Приходи́те<sup>i</sup> к нам в го́сти в суббо́ту. 2 Откро́йте<sup>p</sup> окно́, пожа́луйста. 3 – Мо́жно закури́ть<sup>p</sup> ('to light a cigarette')? – Пожа́луйста, кури́те<sup>i</sup>. 4 Отве́тьте<sup>p</sup> на два вопро́са. 5 Да́йте<sup>p</sup> мне ваш телефо́н. 6 Бери́те<sup>i</sup> пирожо́к. 7 Не уходи́те<sup>i</sup>.

### EXERCISE 15/5

Translate, using the imperative forms:

1 Phone<sup>p</sup> me tomorrow. 2 Could you tell<sup>p</sup> me where the station is? 3 Don't forget<sup>p</sup> to bring<sup>p</sup> the juice. 4 Write<sup>i</sup> to us often. 5 Do have a seat<sup>i</sup>. 6 Would you give<sup>p</sup> me two tickets, please? 6 Don't open<sup>i</sup> the window. This room is cold.

### 15.4 Imperative Exceptions

There aren't many exceptions. Of the verbs we have met so far, only the following need to be noted:

пить<sup>i</sup> 'to drink' has пей(те) 'drink'

All prefixed forms of пить (e.g. вы́пить<sup>p</sup> 'to have a drink') have the same ending:

Вы́пейте<sup>p</sup>. — Have a drink.

The imperative of both е́хать<sup>i</sup> (е́ду, е́дешь) and пое́хать<sup>p</sup> 'to go by transport' is поезжа́й(те)<sup>i/p</sup>.
