дома́ houses — дом<u>а́м</u> (до́м 'house')
де́вушки girls — де́вушк<u>ам</u> (де́вушка 'girl')
пи́сьма letters — пи́сьм<u>ам</u> (письмо́ 'letter')
имена́ names — имен<u>а́м</u> (и́мя 'name')

(b) If the nom. plural ends -и or -я, replace the и or я with ям:

друзья́ friends — друзь<u>я́м</u> 'to friends' (друг 'friend')
ма́тери mothers — матер<u>я́м</u> 'to mothers' (мать 'mother')
неде́ли weeks — неде́л<u>ям</u> 'to weeks' (неде́ля 'week')

### 12.11 The Last Two Spelling Rules (3 and 4)

_Rule 3._ After г, к, х, ч, ж, ш, щ, ц (all the eight letters involved in Rules 1 and 2), you always find <u>а</u> not <u>я</u>. So the dative plural of кни́га 'book' (pl. кни́ги) is кни́г<u>ам</u> (not кни́гям). 'They are learning Russian' from the говори́ть-type verb учи́ть 'to learn' is они́ у́чат (_not_ у́чят) ру́сский язы́к.

_Rule 4._ After those same eight letters, you always find у, not ю. So 'I am learning Russian' is я у<u>чу́</u> (not учю́) ру́сский язы́к.

### 12.12 The Main Uses of the Dative

(1) To translate _to_ a person in sentences such as 'I wrote _to my friend_' (Я писа́л дру́гу – у is the dative ending), 'I shall give ten dollars to the waiter' (Я дам де́сять до́лларов официа́нт<u>у</u>). The dative is the opposite of от + genitive 'from'.

(2) In impersonal constructions with words like на́до 'it is necessary', мо́жно 'it's possible', the person affected is in the dative, e.g. Ему́ мо́жно 'He's allowed to' (lit. 'To him it is possible'). See Lesson 14 for the details.
