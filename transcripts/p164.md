question of how you started to learn Russian and what other languages you know:

to study: изуча́ть<sup>i</sup> +acc. – я изуча́ю, ты изуча́ешь
or
to learn учи́ть<sup>i</sup> +acc. – я учу́, ты у́чишь, они́ у́чат (note у (not ю) and а (not я) after ч – Spelling Rules 3 and 4 (12.11)

Я изуча́ю ру́сский язы́к уже́ пять ме́сяцев. — I have been studying Russian for five months (lit. 'I am studying Russian language already five months').
Я учу́ ру́сский язы́к уже́ шесть лет. — I have been learning Russian for six years.

Both изуча́ть<sup>i</sup> and учи́ть<sup>i</sup> mean 'to study', but учи́ть, like 'to learn', has connotations of memorizing. Both verbs can be used of learning a language. But 'I'm learning these words' (i.e. committing them to memory) is Я учу́ э́ти слова́, while 'She's studying mathematics' (not 'She's learning mathematics') should be Она́ изуча́ет матема́тику.

The perfective of изуча́ть<sup>i</sup> is изучи́ть<sup>p</sup> (изучу́, изу́чишь) and the perfective of учи́ть<sup>i</sup> is вы́учить<sup>p</sup> (вы́учу, вы́учишь). Since perfective verbs always describe a successful, completed act, изучи́ть<sup>p</sup> and вы́учить<sup>p</sup> correspond to 'to master', 'to learn successfully':

Мэ́ри вы́учила<sup>p</sup> ру́сский язы́к. — Mary mastered Russian.
Бори́с Ка́рлович изучи́л<sup>p</sup> эконо́мику А́нглии. — Boris Karlovich has made a thorough study of the British economy.

_Note:_ If you want to say that you're studying without saying _what_ you're studying, use the reflexive verb учи́ть<u>ся</u> 'to study' (somewhere):

Я учу́<u>сь</u> в Ло́ндоне. — I'm studying in London.
